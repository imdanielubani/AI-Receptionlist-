# Non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT)
Last verified: 2026-10-20 · Countries covered: US (Illinois detail)

Door-to-door rides for people who need help getting to medical care: dialysis, doctor visits, therapy, surgery, and hospital discharges. Level of service is **ambulatory** (walks, maybe with a cane or walker), **wheelchair** (lift or ramp van), or **stretcher**. Payers are **Medicaid** (directly, or through a broker), **private pay** (families) and **facilities** (hospitals, nursing homes). It is *not* an ambulance service: no medical care happens in transit.

## Who calls and how it sounds
- **Riders**, often older, disabled or recovering from surgery. They speak slowly, may be hard of hearing or anxious, and give details out of order. They say "ride", "pick me up", "my van", "my driver", "the wheelchair van". They don't say "NEMT" or "ambulatory".
- **Family and caregivers** booking for a parent: "I need to get my dad to dialysis three times a week." They're often price-shopping for private pay.
- **Facilities:** hospital discharge planners, social workers, case managers, nursing homes and dialysis centers. They're rushed and professional, often need a ride the **same day**, and use terms like "discharge", "wheelchair", "stretcher", "pickup at 2".
- **Brokers and plans** (ModivCare, MTM and others) assigning or checking trips.
- **Riders waiting right now:** "Where's my driver?", "Nobody came", "I'm done at the clinic, come get me" (a "will-call" return). This is the most stressful call type.
- Also: job seekers (drivers), vendors and spam.

**Peaks:** morning pickup windows, afternoon return trips, and disruptions like traffic or weather. Missed calls cluster at peaks, and every missed call breeds repeat calls ([Spedsta](https://spedsta.com/blog/the-hidden-cost-of-missed-calls-in-nemt-and-paratransit-programs/)).

## Call reasons (by frequency)
| Reason | Freq | Receptionist handles | Hands off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book a ride (one-way, round trip or recurring) | High | Collect the full trip request (see Booking) | Dispatcher confirms vehicle and time |
| Where's my driver / late / no-show | High at peaks | Calm the caller, get name, pickup address and appointment time | **Transfer to dispatch now**, or an urgent alert after hours |
| Ready for return pickup ("will-call") | Medium | Get name, location and ready time | Transfer or urgent alert to dispatch |
| Hospital discharge, same day | Medium | Collect facility, contact, patient, discharge time, level of service, destination and payer | Urgent: transfer or alert |
| Cancel or change a ride | Medium | Take name, date, time and the change; give the cancellation policy | Dispatcher updates the schedule |
| Price for private pay | Medium | Quote only owner-approved rates | Otherwise the team quotes |
| "Do you take Medicaid / my insurance?" | Medium | Give the owner-approved answer | Eligibility check by the team |
| Service area / "do you go to X?" | Medium | Answer from the county list | Edge cases go to the team |
| Hours, wheelchair, companions, long-distance, airport | Low–Med | FAQ answers | — |
| Complaints | Low | De-escalate and take details | Owner |
| Driver jobs, vendors | Low | Take a message, or redirect to email | — |

Late pickups and no-shows are the top complaint in state NEMT audits. Wisconsin counted 55,320 pickups more than 15 minutes late and 4,154 provider no-shows in about a year ([Wisconsin LAB](https://legis.wisconsin.gov/LaB/reports/15-4highlights_m.pdf)). Riders left waiting outside, and drivers who never called, also show up in complaint logs ([Indiana FSSA](https://WWW.IN.GOV/fssa/ompp/files/Nov-2020-Complaints-Appeals-Detail.pdf)).

## Booking requirements
Collect these for a ride request. State programs ask for roughly the same set ([CT DSS](https://portal.ct.gov/DSS/Health-And-Home-Care/Non-Emergency-Medical-Transportation/FAQ), [TennCare](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/tenncare/documents/NEMTHowToScheduleARide.pdf), [MO DSS](https://dss.mo.gov/mhd/participants/pdf/non-emergency-medical-transportation.pdf)).

**About the rider and caller**
- Rider's full name, and the caller's name and relationship if different.
- Callback number.

**About the trip**
- Pickup address, including apartment or unit, gate code and where to wait.
- Destination name and address, e.g. the clinic or dialysis center.
- Appointment date and time, so pickup can be worked out backwards.
- One-way or round trip. For the return: a set time, or "will-call" (the rider phones when done).
- Recurring schedule, e.g. dialysis Mon/Wed/Fri.

**About the rider's needs**
- **Mobility:** walks; cane or walker; own wheelchair (manual or power); needs a wheelchair provided; scooter; stretcher.
- **Special needs:** oxygen, bariatric, sedation or anesthesia (they need an adult to ride with them), a minor riding (needs a guardian), stairs at pickup.
- **Companions:** caregiver or companion riding along, and how many.

**About payment**
- Payer: Medicaid (and which plan, plus member ID), private pay, or facility.

**Norms**
- About 48 hours' notice is common for Medicaid plans ([Dream Care Rides, provider blog: unverified](https://dreamcarerides.com/blog/illinois-medicaid-transportation-complete-guide)). Missouri requires 3 business days in rural counties.
- Discharges are the exception: commonly picked up within **3 hours** of the facility's call ([Louisiana Medicaid](https://www.ldh.la.gov/assets/medicaid/PC-PM/9.24.21/MedicalTransportation10.2SchedulingandAuthorization09.24.21.pdf), [NC UHC/MTM](https://ams-gateway.uhcprovider.com/content/dam/provider/docs/public/commplan/nc/news/NC-UHCCP-NEMT-Transition.pdf)).
- Many programs say children 14 and under can't ride without a parent or guardian ([Wellpoint TX](https://www.wellpoint.com/content/dam/digital/wellpoint/documents/medicaid/texas/Nonemergency-Medical-Transportation-(NEMT)-Services-(English)-TX.pdf)).
- A ride is a **request until dispatch confirms** a vehicle. The receptionist must never promise a vehicle or an exact pickup time on its own.

**Medicaid in Illinois:** it's a mixed model.
- **Managed-care members** book through their plan's transportation broker (ModivCare, MTM, others), using the number on their card. The broker assigns the provider.
- **Fee-for-service members** can contact an enrolled provider directly. Prior authorization goes through NETSPAP/Transdev for HFS.

Sources conflict on the details ([MediRoutes](https://mediroutes.com/nemt-state-guide/illinois), [Dream Care Rides](https://dreamcarerides.com/blog/illinois-medicaid-transportation-complete-guide), [Waiver Group](https://help.waivergroup.com/en_US/guide-to-becoming-an-adult-health-transportation-provider-in-illinois)). Always ask the owner how Medicaid callers should be handled.

**Typical Illinois rates** (Medicaid ranges, 2025): ambulatory about $35 base + $2.50–8 per loaded mile; wheelchair about $70 base; stretcher about $180 base ([MediRoutes](https://mediroutes.com/nemt-state-guide/illinois)). Private-pay prices vary by provider, so quote only owner-approved numbers.

## Common software and integrations
- **Dispatch and scheduling:** TobiCloud, RouteGenie, TripMaster, Bambi, MediRoutes, NEMT Cloud Dispatch, about $50–200 per vehicle per month ([NextBillion](https://nextbillion.ai/feeds/blog/nemt-scheduling-software-transparent-pricing), [Upper](https://www.upperinc.com/blog/best-nemt-dispatch-software/)). Public APIs are rare or undocumented, so check per vendor before planning a direct-booking function.
- **Small operators** often run on a phone, a web form and a spreadsheet or calendar, with no software to integrate.
- **Default for small operators: "trip request" mode.** The receptionist captures the full request, the owner or dispatcher is alerted immediately, and the team calls back to confirm. Use calendar booking (Cal.com) only if the owner truly books fixed slots, which is rare in NEMT.

## Transfers, escalation and emergencies
- **Medical emergency on the line** (chest pain, can't breathe, unconscious, fall with injury, stroke signs, severe bleeding): "Please hang up and call 911 now." Say this first. NEMT is not an emergency service, and providers say so in their FAQs.
- **Rider waiting and no driver / driver late / stranded after an appointment:** urgent. Transfer to dispatch during operating hours. After hours, send an urgent alert to the on-call person and tell the caller when they'll hear back. Never leave a stranded rider with only "we'll call you tomorrow".
- **Same-day hospital discharge:** urgent. Transfer, or send an urgent alert.
- **Complaints about a driver or a safety incident:** transfer to the owner or take a detailed message. Don't argue or admit fault.
- **Messages only:** job applications, vendors, general questions the receptionist can't answer, and non-urgent booking requests after hours.

## Never say / compliance
- **No medical advice:** don't say whether someone is "fit to travel" or "needs an ambulance". If unsure, tell them to ask their doctor; in an emergency, 911.
- **Never confirm a booking or pickup time** that dispatch hasn't confirmed. Say "I've got your request in. The team will call to confirm your pickup time."
- **No price quotes** beyond owner-approved rates. Never promise Medicaid will cover a trip; eligibility is checked by the team.
- **Privacy (HIPAA):**
  - An NEMT provider handling Medicaid or facility trips is typically a HIPAA **business associate** ([lovable.dev guide: weak source](https://lovable.dev/en/guides/how-to-start-a-medical-transportation-business)). Treat all rider info (health conditions, appointments, Medicaid ID) as protected.
  - Collect only what the trip needs.
  - Don't read trip details back to unverified callers: get the rider's name plus the pickup address or date first.
  - Don't email transcripts. Send dashboard links.
  - Use post-call PII scrubbing.
- **Call recording (Illinois):** Illinois is an **all-party consent** state for recording private conversations (720 ILCS 5/14-2) ([recordinglaw.com](https://www.recordinglaw.com/party-two-party-consent-states/illinois-recording-laws/audio/), [Chicago business attorney](https://www.businessattorneychicago.com/can-illinois-businesses-record-phone-calls-with-ai-note-takers/)). Announce recording in the greeting, before the caller says anything substantive.
  - BIPA covers voiceprints. Don't enable any voice-ID or caller-voice cloning features.
- **AI disclosure:** Illinois has no specific statute, per a 2026 tracker ([PyAI](https://pyai.com/voice-ai-compliance/laws)). Best practice: say it's a virtual assistant in the greeting, and never claim to be human when asked.
- **Phrasing that loses customers:** rushing elderly callers, asking for the same detail twice, jargon ("ambulatory level of service"), or "you need to call your broker" with no help on how.

## Phrasing that works
- **Greeting:** "Thanks for calling <Business>. I'm the virtual assistant, and this call is recorded. How can I help you today?"
- **Mobility, in plain words:** "Will they be walking to the van, or do they ride in a wheelchair?"
- **Confirming:** "Let me read that back. Pickup at 412 Oak Street, apartment 2, on Tuesday the 21st, going to the dialysis center on Main, for a 9 a.m. appointment, wheelchair, and back home afterwards. Is that right?"
- **Request, not booking:** "I've passed your ride request to our team. They'll call you at this number to confirm the pickup time."
- **Late driver:** "I'm sorry you're waiting. Let me get you to our dispatcher right now."
- **Emergency:** "That sounds like an emergency. Please hang up and call 911 right away."
- **Can't answer:** "I don't have that information, but I'll have the team call you back about it."
- **Closing:** "You're all set on my end. Anything else I can help with?"

## Default call flow for this business type
1. Greeting with AI and recording disclosure. Check whether open.
2. Work out what they need:
   - **Book a ride:** collect the trip request, read it back, alert the team, and promise a confirmation call.
   - **Where's my driver / ready for pickup:** collect name, location and time. During hours, transfer to dispatch. After hours, send an urgent alert and say when they'll hear back.
   - **Same-day discharge (facility):** collect facility, contact, patient, discharge time, mobility, destination and payer. Urgent alert, plus transfer during hours.
   - **Cancel or change:** collect name, date and the change; give the policy; alert the team.
   - **Questions** (area, Medicaid, wheelchair, companions, hours, prices): FAQ.
   - **Medical emergency:** tell them to call 911 first.
   - **Complaint:** de-escalate, take a message, transfer to the owner during hours.
   - **Wants a person:** offer help once, then transfer during hours, or take a message.
   - **Spam, sales or jobs:** take a short message or end politely.
3. Recap what happens next, then end the call.

## Owner question bank
Ranked by harm if wrong. The suggested default is in brackets.
1. **Who handles live ride problems** (late driver, stranded rider, same-day discharge), and their number. [Transfer to the owner's mobile during operating hours. After hours, an urgent text and email alert.]
2. **Real operating hours**, for the office and for rides, including weekends. [Office Mon–Fri business hours; rides by arrangement.]
3. **How Medicaid callers are handled:** book directly, or send them through their plan's broker. [Take their plan and member ID; the team checks coverage before confirming.]
4. **How requests get confirmed:** who, how fast, where alerts go, and any dispatch software. [The team calls back within 2 business hours; email + text alerts; no software.]
5. **Private-pay prices the assistant may quote.** [None; the team quotes when confirming.]
6. **Notice, same-day and cancellation policy.** [48 hours preferred; same-day if possible; please cancel 24 hours ahead.]
7. Stretcher and bariatric service, and long-distance limits. [Don't offer stretcher unless listed.]
8. Languages other than English. [English only.]
